History of Braille

Louis Braille was born in 1809 in Coupvray, France. As a child he lost his sight due to an accident, a situation that inspired him to develop a system that would allow people with visual impairments to read and write independently.


At just fifteen years old he created the Braille system, based on a cell of six raised dots. Thanks to this innovation, millions of people around the world have been able to access education, culture and communication without depending on others.

How does Braille work?

The Braille system uses a cell made up of six dots arranged in two columns of three dots each.


Depending on which dots are raised, letters, numbers, punctuation marks, mathematical symbols and even musical scores are represented.


Reading is done using the sense of touch, gently sliding the fingers over each cell.

Where is Braille used?

The Braille system is part of the daily life of millions of people with visual impairments. Thanks to it, they can access important information, study, work and get around with greater independence.


  • Books, magazines and libraries.
  • Schools, universities and educational centers.
  • Medication packaging and labels.
  • Hospitals and health centers.
  • Train stations, subways and public transportation.
  • Room, elevator and building signage.
  • ATMs and payment terminals.
  • Computers and devices with Braille displays.
  • Adapted musical scores.
  • Election material and official documents.
