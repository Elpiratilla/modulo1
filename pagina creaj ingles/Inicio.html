What is the Braille system?

Braille is a tactile reading and writing system used by people with visual impairments. It was created by Louis Braille in the 19th century.


It is made up of a cell of six raised dots. Depending on which dots are raised, letters, numbers, punctuation marks and symbols are represented.


Today Braille is used all over the world and allows millions of people to study, work and communicate independently.

History of the Braille System

1809

Birth

Louis Braille was born on January 4, 1809 in Coupvray, France.

1812

Accident

At the age of three he suffered an accident to one eye. The infection spread and he completely lost his sight.

1821

Inspiration

Charles Barbier presented a night-writing system that inspired Louis Braille.

1824

Creation

At just 15 years old he created the Braille system, using a six-dot cell.

1829

First book

He published the first book explaining how the Braille system works.

1852

Death

Louis Braille passed away in Paris, but his invention continued to spread throughout the world.

1952

Recognition

His remains were moved to the Pantheon in Paris as a tribute to his legacy.
